# Narrative: Message, Purpose, Business Logic

This workstream defines the story logic the user is trying to put into the deck.

## Starting point

The user is not asking for slides. The user is asking for an outcome:

- approval
- alignment
- funding
- understanding
- action

The presentation exists to change what an audience knows, believes, or decides.

## Narrative spine

Every serious deck should be able to answer these questions:

1. What is happening?
2. Why does it matter now?
3. What is the key insight or recommendation?
4. What proof supports it?
5. What should the audience do next?

If the deck cannot answer these cleanly, more design will not save it.

## Story structure

### Context

Set the world fast. Only include context the audience needs in order to interpret the recommendation.

### Tension

Make the problem or opportunity legible. What is misaligned, risky, stalled, expensive, or underexploited?

### Insight

Reveal the central interpretation. This is the slide or sequence where the audience understands what the evidence means.

### Recommendation

State what should happen. Do not leave the decision implicit.

### Proof

Use data, examples, prototypes, or comparisons to justify the move.

### Close

End with the ask, next step, or decision required.

## Narrative guardrails

- Do not bury the recommendation after long setup.
- Do not let proof slides contradict earlier framing.
- Do not let appendix content leak into the main argument.
- Do not confuse "interesting background" with "needed premise."

## Professional use-case differences

### Executive update

- shorter story
- decision-oriented
- fewer details on slide, more in appendix

### Investor or pitch deck

- more emphasis on momentum, market belief, and why now
- tighter arc from problem to solution to proof

### Technical review

- deeper evidence density
- architecture and tradeoff slides carry more weight
- claims must survive expert scrutiny

### Product or roadmap review

- connect user pain, product choice, sequencing, and impact
- make tradeoffs explicit

## What the skill should enforce

- identify the audience and desired change before writing slides
- maintain a clear thread from title to appendix
- keep the story consistent when slides are added or removed
- know when a deck is really two decks and should be split
