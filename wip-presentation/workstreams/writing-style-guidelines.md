# Writing Style: Clear, Crisp, Useful

This workstream adapts the `edit-write-rewrite` mindset to slide writing.

## Writing objective

Presentation writing should reduce reader effort while increasing confidence in the message. It should feel crisp, grounded, and easy to say aloud.

## Core rules

- Lead with the point.
- Keep one paragraph or one slide to one job.
- Preserve the strongest evidence and tradeoffs.
- Cut filler, throat-clearing, and inflated setup.
- Do not sound polished at the cost of meaning.

## Slide-title rules

- Titles should be conclusions, not labels.
- A title should usually read like a sentence fragment or full sentence with a claim.
- A good title answers: what should the audience take away here?
- If the title can be swapped with five others, it is too generic.

Examples:

- weak: `Market Overview`
- stronger: `Enterprise demand is concentrating in two segments with faster renewal cycles`

- weak: `Product Strategy`
- stronger: `We should narrow the launch to one workflow to improve adoption and proof density`

## Body-copy rules

- Support the title; do not repeat it.
- Use bullets only when parallel structure is real.
- Keep bullets short enough to scan but specific enough to matter.
- Replace vague adjectives with concrete implications.
- When a caveat matters, say it plainly.

## Speaker-note rules

- Notes should help the presenter interpret the slide, not read it verbatim.
- Use notes to store transitions, nuance, and optional elaboration.
- Keep on-slide copy for what the audience should see; keep notes for what the presenter should say.

## Compression rules

- Cut repeated setup first.
- Cut ornamental transition lines.
- Replace multiple weak bullets with one stronger sentence where possible.
- Split overloaded slides instead of shrinking text to fit.

## Tone

- direct
- calm
- precise
- confident without overclaiming

## Red flags

- consultant-sounding phrases with no operational meaning
- bullets that all begin with abstract nouns
- titles that merely name a topic area
- invented certainty where evidence is mixed
- copy that reads well in isolation but does not advance the deck

## What the skill should do

- rewrite rough input into action titles and proof points
- preserve meaning while reducing drag
- keep the deck readable in skim mode
- maintain a consistent voice across slides, notes, and appendix material
