WIP Presentation Skill

Build decks like a narrative architect, not a box generator.

This map turns the current research into a working model for a presentation skill. The emphasis is narrative quality first, then design discipline, then PPTX mechanics.

5 Workstreams
4 Professional use cases
6 Quality gates

Anti-Slop Rules

Narrative before layout
Action titles carry the story
Prototype before PPTX
One slide, one job
Editable text and charts
No generic AI deck patterns

Central thesis: the real failure mode is weak story logic, not merely weak styling.

Professional Use Cases

Different presentation jobs need different evidence and pacing.

Executive Update

Decision support, not status theater.

Audience: leadership team
Desired outcome: approve direction or remove a blocker
Failure mode: informative slides with no decision spine
Technical Review

Claims must survive expert scrutiny.

Audience: engineers and technical leadership
Desired outcome: confidence in design and tradeoffs
Failure mode: story collapses when someone asks for proof
Pitch Narrative

Belief, urgency, and why-now have to land fast.

Audience: investors or external decision-makers
Desired outcome: commitment to the next step
Failure mode: polished visuals without conviction
Product Story

User pain, sequencing, and tradeoffs must connect.

Audience: cross-functional product partners
Desired outcome: align on priorities and roadmap logic
Failure mode: too many parallel stories dilute the thesis

Default Flow

Prototype the argument before building the deck.

1. Discover

Audience, purpose, stakes, decision

The brief has to identify who must be moved and what must happen.

2. Narrative

Message, titles, slide roles

Shape the argument until the deck can be skimmed as a story.

3. JSON

Structured prototype data

Model the deck as a cheap planning surface before any PPTX work.

4. Artifact

Inspectable visual map

Render sequence, density, and relationships so flaws appear early.

5. Build

Editable PPTX authoring

Author or update the actual deck only after the story shape holds.

6. Verify

Quality plus integrity

Check visible fidelity, editable structure, and anti-slop gates.

Job Phases

The skill should move through disciplined phases.

Briefing

Clarify the ask.

Purpose, audience, presenter, and time constraint are explicit.

Structuring

Lock the deck logic.

Control the narrative spine before exploring visual treatments.

Prototyping

Review the shape cheaply.

Use JSON and an inspectable artifact to stress the structure.

Authoring

Build the editable deck.

Turn the approved structure into a working `.pptx` document.

Verification

Prove it survives handoff.

Confirm layout, titles, editability, and quality gates still hold.

Workstreams

Five capabilities have to cooperate for the skill to feel professional.

Narrative

Purpose, message, business logic

The deck must know what it is trying to change in the audience.

Writing

Action titles and crisp support copy

The text has to be easy to skim, easy to say, and hard to misread.

Design

Hierarchy, grids, evidence display, motion

The style should feel chosen for the message, not copied from a template farm.

Technical

PPTX CRUD and OOXML awareness

The deck has to be editable, verifiable, and safe to update later.
